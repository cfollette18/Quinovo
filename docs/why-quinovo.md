# Why Quinovo

An operational ontology is the nouns and verbs of a business — the things it has and the things it does. The closed, consultant-driven approach models your business for you, rents you a frontier model, and locks the integration layer behind a proprietary marketplace.

Quinovo makes a different bet. Six things make it Quinovo and not that.

## 1. Discovered, not hand-built

The engine never knows your domain. A language model proposes the types, rules, and actions from your live data. You only approve or turn down. No consultants. No ontology authored by hand. The ontology is *discovered* from the data, not built by experts.

## 2. MCP is the connector layer

No proprietary connector marketplace. Any MCP-compatible client — Cursor, Claude, Hermes, your own agents — reads and writes the ontology. Quinovo also exposes itself as MCP. Bidirectional. The integration layer is a protocol, not a moat.

## 3. Small specialists, not one giant model

Filter the ontology into a dataset and train a small specialized model on just this workspace's data (the Train tab). Specialists are cheap, private, fast, and owned by you — not rented from a frontier lab. The frontier model authors the ontology; the specialist lives inside it.

## 4. HITL is the only human job

The loop runs itself. Below 0.8 confidence parks for your review; 0.8 and above auto-applies. Humans are approvers, not builders. There are no "Tick" or "Run inference" buttons in the UI — the loop just runs.

## 5. Open source

Apache-2.0. The ontology kernel is yours — read it, run it, fork it, ship it. No enterprise sales call.

## 6. Security is a primitive

Data, Logic, Action, Security. Four primitives. Security is first-class in the kernel — guards, policies, seed tuples — evaluated at call time, not bolted on after the fact.

---

## The loop

1. **Data flows in** through MCP-style sources.
2. **The model proposes** types, rules, and actions from that data.
3. **You approve** what it's not sure about (below 0.8). The rest applies on its own.
4. **Actions write back** to real systems through MCP.

Analytics — the graph, the catalog, the inference queue, the audit log, the train jobs — are by-products of the loop, not separately hand-built dashboards.

This is the operational ontology, the Quinovo way: discovered, open, small-model, human-approval-first, secure by primitive.
